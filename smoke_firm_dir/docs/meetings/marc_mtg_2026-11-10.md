### Nested-logit equation for the chosen specification

Using the notation in the [previous presented troubleshooting notes](../presented/sigma_troubleshooting_marc_2026-10-14.md):

$$
\log(s_{it}/s_{0t})
= c + \alpha  p_{it} + \beta_a a_{it}
+ \sigma\log(s_{it}/s_{gt}) + \xi_{it}.
$$

$i$ indexes national incumbent brands and $t$ months. $p_{it}$ is the liquid-volume-weighted real price per mL, $a_{it}$ is delivered nicotine concentration, and $\xi_{it}$ is the unobserved demand term. Intercept $c$ is included.

The shares use constant-intensity market size:

$$
M_t=\bar{k}\,\mathrm{Adults}_t,\qquad
s_{it}=q_{it}/M_t,\qquad
s_{gt}=Q_t/M_t,\qquad
s_{0t}=1-Q_t/M_t,
$$

where $q_{it}$ is eligible brand liquid volume, $Q_t$ sums eligible incumbent and fringe volumes, and $\bar{k}$ is the full-period mean of the monthly NCP purchasing-household consumption-intensity series. Constant intensity does not mean constant market size: adult population varies over time.

The estimated equation, with coefficients rounded, is:

$$
\log(s_{it}/s_{0t})
= -6.8938 - 0.3336\,p_{it} + 0.1100\,a_{it}
+ 0.8695\log(s_{it}/s_{gt}) + \widehat{\xi}_{it}.
$$

Price and within-nest log share are jointly instrumented by **sales-weighted state tax and annual national incumbent count**. Current quality is treated as exogenous. The two excluded instruments identify the two endogenous regressors exactly; standard errors are clustered by month.

The negative conditional tax–price relationship remains unresolved.  $\sigma$ 's conventional 95% interval extends above 1, and the constant-intensity market-size assumption still requires substantive support.

## 3. Supply-side setup: cost recovery and fringe demand

### Decisions before coding

Start by recovering demand residuals and marginal costs at observed prices, then implement the static pricing fixed point manually and test observed-market replication. Do not use packaged black-box equilibrium solvers. The planned code folder is `code/4_static_pricing_subgame/`. Treat each brand as a vertically integrated pricing firm, as agreed: it chooses its representative retail price and receives the corresponding retail revenue. Recovered marginal costs are effective integrated-channel costs; operating profits are integrated-channel profits, rather than separately identified manufacturer profits. No separate wholesale-price or retailer-margin stage is modeled. This is the initial approximation for the static subgame and must remain explicit when interpreting later entry/exit results.

### Fringe utility includes unobserved quality

**Decision: allow fringe mean utility to include unobserved quality, rather than restricting it to measured nicotine quality alone.** In the signed- $\alpha$  convention, the full mean utility is

$$
\delta_{F,t}=c+\beta_a a_{F,t}+\alpha  p_{F,t}+\xi_{F,t},\qquad \alpha <0.
$$

The fringe is one aggregate alternative in the same e-cigarette nest. For the initial observed-market replication, calibrate its mean utility from observed shares:

$$
\delta_{F,t}^{\mathrm{obs}}
=\log(s_{F,t}/s_{0t})
-\sigma\log(s_{F,t}/s_{gt}).
$$

Given consistently defined observed fringe price and quality, recover the implied unobserved component as

$$
\xi_{F,t}=\delta_{F,t}^{\mathrm{obs}}-c-\beta_a a_{F,t}-\alpha  p_{F,t}.
$$

For the first static solve, it is sufficient to use calibrated total fringe utility; separating price, quality, and the residual requires consistent fringe aggregates. Calibration matches the observed aggregate share but does not estimate a separate fringe demand equation or validate the assumed aggregation.

Fringe utility may vary across months. Hold it fixed within each month's incumbent pricing game: the fringe remains passive, with no strategic price choice. ## 4. Manual static pricing fixed point: observed-market replication

Completed October 28, 2026. Use the recovered October 27 costs and demand states from Section 3, with the full 540 incumbent rows in 132 monthly markets. Brands are vertically integrated single-product pricing firms. Incumbent quality, residuals, marginal costs, roster, and fringe total utility are fixed within each market; all incumbent and fringe shares are recomputed at each trial price vector.

### Algorithm

Implement simultaneous damped markup substitution directly, without an equilibrium/root/optimization package:

$$
T_i(p)=\widehat{mc}_i-\frac{1}{\widehat\alpha  A_i(p)},\qquad
p_i^{(k+1)}=(1-\lambda)p_i^{(k)}+\lambda T_i(p^{(k)}),
$$

$$
A_i(p)=\frac{1}{1-\widehat\sigma}
-\frac{\widehat\sigma}{1-\widehat\sigma}s_{i\mid g}(p)-s_i(p).
$$

Four starting vectors: marginal cost + $0.10/mL, +$1/mL, +$5/mL, and heterogeneous positive cost increments. Cross these with damping values 0.25, 0.5 and 1: **12 attempts per month, 1,584 attempts total**. No observed price vector is used as a starting vector or convergence target. The reference solution is fixed in advance as cost+$1 with damping 0.5.

Require all three: maximum price step < 1e-10, maximum undamped map residual < 1e-9, and maximum scaled FOC residual < 1e-8. Cap at 2,000 iterations and report nonconvergence explicitly. The scaled FOC is `1 + (price - mc) *  $\alpha$  * A`, avoiding dependence of the stopping rule on the volume units. Save every iteration's diagnostics. This is a markup fixed point, not an exact best-response iteration.

### Results

**All 1,584 attempts converged to the same observed-price solution within numerical tolerance.** The largest observed-price discrepancy across all starts/damping values is below **3e-10 real dollars/mL**. The predeclared reference solutions differ from observed prices by less than 1e-10.

| Damping | Attempts / converged | Median iterations | Maximum iterations |
|----------------|---------------------:|------------------:|-------------------:|
| 0.25 | 528 / 528 | 73 | 82 |
| 0.50 | 528 / 528 | 32 | 36 |
| 1.00 (undamped) | 528 / 528 | 57 | 1,335 |

Damping 0.5 is the practical starting choice for these observed markets; it is not a general convergence guarantee for counterfactual markets.

### Unilateral profit-deviation checks

For every one of the 540 reference incumbent solutions, hold rivals' prices fixed and find its best own price by **manually coded bracketing and bisection** on the own-profit FOC. This is separate from the multi-firm fixed-point solve.

For own price at or above marginal cost, `A_i > 0` and its own-price derivative is positive. Since  $\alpha$  < 0, `g_i = 1 +  $\alpha$ *(p_i-mc_i)*A_i` is strictly decreasing: it starts at 1 at cost and becomes negative at sufficiently high price. Its unique zero maximizes own variable profit; below-cost profits are nonpositive and profit tends to zero at infinite price. Thus the manually bracketed root checks the global unilateral optimum under the maintained single-product demand model.

The largest best-response price gap is **9.81e-11 dollars/mL**. The maximum apparent profit gain is **4.48e-8 real dollars** (relative gain below **1.35e-14**), consistent with rounding error. Own-profit FOC slopes are negative for all 540 solutions. These checks support numerical Nash optimality for the maintained game; they do not establish uniqueness of the joint equilibrium.

### Interpretation

This confirms that the manual algorithm can reproduce the observed-month equilibrium implied by the recovered demand states and marginal costs. Because costs were inverted from those observed prices, replication is an implementation check, not independent evidence that demand, integrated-channel conduct, instruments, or costs are empirically correct. No Markov process, fixed operating cost, entry/exit decision, or investment is required for this step. The replication exercise does not replace observed inputs or demand estimates; a separate numerical cost-perturbation check is described below.

- **Audit 2: Price, cost, and markup time series** — inspect brand-level levels, jumps, and price–cost gaps. All 540 recovered marginal costs are positive, but positivity alone does not validate the cost estimates.

- **Audit 3: Convergence from MC + $1/mL** — map and scaled-FOC residuals approach zero from starting prices that do not use observed prices. Damping 0.5 is consistently faster in the difficult month. This supports numerical convergence to a stationary solution; iterations are computational updates, not firms learning over calendar time.

  **Pricing first-order condition (FOC) for Audit 3:** each firm chooses its own price to maximize

  $$
  \pi_i(p)=(p_i-mc_i)q_i(p)-FC_i,
  $$

  holding rivals' prices, costs, and non-price demand states fixed. Its interior pricing condition is

  $$
  \frac{\partial\pi_i}{\partial p_i}
  =q_i+(p_i-mc_i)\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial p_i}=0.
  $$

  Since $q_i=M_ts_i$, divide by market size to obtain

  $$
  s_i+(p_i-mc_i)\frac{\partial s_i}{\partial p_i}=0.
  $$

  Our nested logit gives $\partial s_i/\partial p_i=\alpha s_iA_i(p)$, where

  $$
  A_i(p)=\frac{1}{1-\sigma}
  -\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}s_{i\mid g}(p)-s_i(p).
  $$

  Dividing by positive $s_i$ gives the **scaled FOC used in the audit graphs**:

  $$
  \boxed{1+\alpha(p_i-mc_i)A_i(p)=0.}
  $$

  The plotted scaled-FOC residual is the maximum absolute value of this expression across firms at each iteration. Near zero means that the marginal benefit of a higher price balances the profit lost from lower sales. The separate unilateral-profit checks establish maximization under the maintained model; the FOC alone establishes stationarity.

## 5. Forward-looking entry/exit: exploratory transition-bin support

### Question

### Joint nicotine–demand-shock bins

The exercise uses 540 incumbent firm-month observations and 531 consecutive monthly transitions, pooled across brands. Only adjacent calendar months for the same brand count as transitions; roster disappearance is not classified as economic exit.

| 4 × 4 | 16 / 16 | 6 | 12 / 16 |
| 5 × 5 | 24 / 25 | 1 | 11 / 25 |
| 6 × 6 | 36 / 36 | 1 | 9 / 36 |

Departures are observed transitions out of a state: they are the observations supporting one row of the transition matrix, rather than the count for each possible destination. The 20-departure threshold is descriptive, not a statistical adequacy criterion.

### Why use quantile bins?

- Quantile boundaries divide each variable into approximately equally populated groups, giving better coverage than equally spaced intervals when observations are unevenly distributed. Equal marginal bin counts do not guarantee equal counts in the joint states.
- In this sample, the 3 × 3 quantile grid occupies all nine joint states. The equally spaced 3 × 3 grid occupies only seven of nine states, leaving two transition rows unsupported.
- **The 3 × 3 quantile grid is the leading candidate:** all nine states have at least 27 observed departures. Finer grids introduce thinly supported rows; the 4 × 4 grid has a state with only six departures, and the 5 × 5 grid has one with only a single departure.

### Initial transition specification: independent processes, pooled across firms

Following the conference model, maintain separate exogenous first-order Markov processes for nicotine quality and the recovered demand shock:

$$
a_{i,t+1}\sim F_a(\cdot\mid a_{it}),\qquad
\xi_{i,t+1}\sim F_\xi(\cdot\mid\xi_{it}).
$$

Estimate one pooled 3 × 3 nicotine transition matrix and one pooled 3 × 3 demand-shock transition matrix. Maintain conditional independence between the processes, so the implied transition probability between the nine joint states is

$$
\Pr(a',\xi'\mid a,\xi)=P_a(a'\mid a)P_\xi(\xi'\mid\xi).
$$

This gives 12 free transition probabilities rather than the 72 in an unrestricted nine-state matrix. Independence is a maintained simplifying assumption, not a consequence of exogeneity or the occupancy diagnostics. It does not settle dependence across firms or the fringe transition process.

Pool across firms separately for each process: firms in the same nicotine bin share its nicotine transition law, and firms in the same demand-shock bin share its demand-shock transition law. The alternative, firm-specific matrices, uses substantially fewer observations per firm; MARKTEN has only 11 consecutive transitions overall. The exploratory joint-grid exercise found departures from only 2–7 of nine joint states per firm. Those missing joint rows concern unrestricted nine-state matrices; support for each firm's separate three-state matrices must be assessed separately. Pooling is maintained for the initial model and does not establish identical transition behavior across firms.

## 9. Fixed operating cost: common constant to estimate

Maintain one monthly fixed operating cost shared across firms and constant over time:

$$
FC_{it}=FC.
$$

Static equilibrium operating profit, net of this cost, is

$$
\pi_i^*(\Omega)=(p_i^*(\Omega)-mc_i)\bar M s_i^*(\Omega)-FC.
$$

Treat $FC$ as a structural parameter whose value we seek to identify and estimate from the eventual entry/exit specification. Section 25 assigns a provisional solver-trial value; no value has been estimated. Candidate values may be used to test the dynamic solver; those trials are not estimates.

Fixed operating cost is paid for each active month regardless of sales volume. Under the current post-profit exit timing, firms earn that month's variable profit and pay $FC$ before choosing whether to exit. It changes profit levels and continuation incentives, but not the static interior optimal price. It is distinct from marginal cost, sunk entry cost, and exit scrap value.

Identification remains to be established jointly with the other dynamic primitives. Define economic entry/exit and the estimation moments or likelihood before claiming that the common $FC$ can be separately identified from scrap values and entry costs. Historical pricing outputs remain variable profits before fixed operating costs.

## 10. Entry/exit timing: retain the conference draft

Retain post-profit exit decisions and next-month activation of entrants:

1. At the start of the month, active firms observe the current industry state.
2. Incumbents play the static Bertrand pricing game and receive variable profit minus the common monthly fixed operating cost $FC$.
3. After current profits, incumbents choose whether to exit and a potential entrant decides whether to pay the sunk entry cost.
4. Exiting firms are absent next month; continuing firms' states transition; an entrant starts operating next month in its initial state.

The incumbent Bellman equation under this timing is

$$
V_i(\Omega)=\pi_i^*(\Omega)+\max\left\{\phi_i,\;\beta\,\mathbb{E}[V_i(\Omega')\mid\Omega,\ i\text{ continues}]\right\}.
$$

Here $\pi_i^*$ is current equilibrium profit after $FC$, $\phi_i$ is scrap value received at the exit decision date, and $\beta$ discounts next month's value. Both exit and continuation receive today's operating profit, so exit cannot avoid the current month's fixed cost. An entrant pays its sunk entry cost at the decision date and receives no operating profit until the following month.

The expectation must incorporate rivals' equilibrium decisions, entrant activation, and the state transitions. The private payoff draws, discounting, entrant initial-state distribution, and initial roster/cap restriction are specified in Sections 11–14. Entry admission depends on vacancies in the starting roster, as specified in Section 15. The equation records timing; no dynamic equilibrium or payoff parameters have been estimated.

## 11. Private scrap-value and sunk-entry-cost draws

Use privately drawn scrap values and sunk entry costs for the initial dynamic model. Each firm observes its own relevant draw before its entry/exit decision; rivals do not observe that realization. Scrap value is paid upon exit, and sunk entry cost is paid upon entry under the Section 10 timing. The common operating cost $FC$ remains a constant parameter to estimate, not a privately drawn monthly cost.

This selects the private-draw branch discussed in the conference draft. The reconstructed conference document records both fixed/known and private-draw versions as alternatives; this decision resolves that ambiguity in favor of private draws.

An incumbent compares its realized scrap value with discounted continuation value; an entrant compares its realized sunk entry cost with the discounted value of becoming active. Choices conditional on public state generally become probabilities after integrating over the private draws. The Section 10 maximum therefore describes the choice conditional on the relevant realized draw; an ex-ante value function must integrate over that draw, rather than treat scrap value as a known constant.

**Fresh monthly private draws:** each active firm receives a new scrap-value draw each month, and that month's potential entrant receives a new sunk-entry-cost draw. Firms know the relevant distributions and observe their own current realization before deciding, but do not observe rivals' realizations. Draws are not persistent: a current realization does not carry over as a firm's permanent scrap value or entry cost.

For this initial setup, fresh private payoff draws are independent across firms and decision periods and convey no additional information about future public-state transitions. This restriction concerns private payoff draws, not independence of nicotine or demand shocks across firms, which remains a separate decision. Use exponential entry costs and normal net scrap values as specified below; their parameters and the identifying normalization remain to be determined.

Consequently, private payoff draws can be integrated out when calculating the value before the current draw. Writing $C_i(\Omega)=\beta\,\mathbb{E}[\bar V_i(\Omega')\mid\Omega,\ i\text{ continues}]$, the incumbent's ex-ante Bellman equation becomes

$$
\bar V_i(\Omega)=\pi_i^*(\Omega)+\mathbb{E}_{\phi}\left[\max\{\phi,C_i(\Omega)\}\right].
$$

The continuation expectation includes rivals' equilibrium exit probabilities, potential entry, and public-state transitions. The incumbent exits when its realized scrap value exceeds $C_i(\Omega)$. No current private draw needs to be retained as a persistent state variable under this assumption.

The entrant's initial-quality distribution and its revelation after commitment are specified in Section 13. Entry and exit policies will be computed jointly given these information assumptions and the roster/cap rules; no distributions or dynamic payoff parameters have been estimated yet.

### Selected payoff distributions

For the initial model, use exponential sunk entry costs and normally distributed net scrap payoffs:

$$
x_{e,t}\sim\operatorname{Exponential}(\text{mean }\mu_E),\qquad \mu_E>0,
$$

$$
\phi_{i,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_S^2),\qquad \sigma_S>0.
$$

Use common distribution parameters across firms and time, with fresh independent private draws under the information structure above. The entry-cost rate is $1/\mu_E$, so the mean and standard deviation both equal $\mu_E$; writing the mean explicitly avoids a rate/scale ambiguity. Entry costs are nonnegative. Net scrap value may be positive or negative because it represents liquidation proceeds net of shutdown costs, rather than gross asset-sale receipts alone.

**Estimation objective:** seek to estimate the exponential entry-cost parameter $\mu_E$, the mean sunk entry cost (equivalently, rate $1/\mu_E$). There is no separate exponential standard-deviation parameter: its standard deviation equals $\mu_E$. The mean scrap payoff is normalized to zero in Section 16; identification of the remaining parameters still requires assessment.

This is a parsimonious starting specification, not an empirical finding about the distributions. Normalize mean net scrap payoff $\mu_S=0$ as explained in Section 16. Seek to estimate the remaining parameters $FC$, $\mu_E$, and $\sigma_S$; none has yet been estimated; Section 25 assigns provisional trial values. The normalization and distribution families do not by themselves establish identification of those remaining parameters.

For a persistent named potential entrant, let $B_e(\Omega)$ be the discounted expected value of becoming active next month, and let $W_e(\Omega)$ be the discounted expected value of remaining eligible next month. Both expectations incorporate rivals' simultaneous choices. Define $D_e(\Omega)=B_e(\Omega)-W_e(\Omega)$. Entry probability is

$$
p_E(\Omega)=
\begin{cases}
0,&D_e(\Omega)\leq0,\\
1-\exp\{-D_e(\Omega)/\mu_E\},&D_e(\Omega)>0.
\end{cases}
$$

For an incumbent with discounted continuation value $C_i(\Omega)$, exit probability is

$$
p_i^{\mathrm{exit}}(\Omega)
=1-\Phi\!\left(\frac{C_i(\Omega)}{\sigma_S}\right),
$$

where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. These probabilities integrate over each firm's private draw; continuation and entry values still depend on equilibrium behavior and the remaining model decisions. No dynamic equilibrium has been solved.

## 16. Scrap-mean normalization and remaining estimation parameters

### Selected normalization

Normalize mean net scrap value to zero:

$$
\mu_S=0,\qquad \phi_{i,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_S^2).
$$

Seek to estimate the common monthly operating cost $FC$, exponential mean industry-entry cost $\mu_E>0$, and net scrap standard deviation $\sigma_S>0$. The exponential standard deviation equals its mean and is not an additional parameter. No numerical trial values or estimates have been chosen yet.

Zero mean is an identifying/modeling restriction, not a finding that average observed liquidation proceeds are zero. Scrap is a net exit payoff, so the distribution still permits positive and negative realizations. Estimates of operating and entry costs must be interpreted conditional on this normalization.

### Why restrict the payoff location?

Entry and exit choices compare continuation values with entry and exit payoffs. Without payoff-location restrictions, shifts in operating costs, scrap payoffs, and sunk entry costs can offset one another. To illustrate, let $r_i(\Omega)$ be variable profit before $FC$, and consider a common constant $d$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
FC'&=FC+(1-\beta)d, & \phi'&=\phi-\beta d,\\
x_e'&=x_e-\beta d, & \bar V_i'&=\bar V_i-d.
\end{aligned}
$$

These transformations preserve the Bellman equation and entry/exit comparisons when the corresponding shifted payoff distributions are admissible. Discounted continuation value and scrap both fall by $\beta d$, and the entrant's discounted value and entry cost both fall by $\beta d$, leaving the same choices. Thus observed choices alone need not determine all payoff locations without additional restrictions.

Our exponential entry-cost family already restricts support to start at zero: a shifted exponential generally lies outside that chosen family. Consequently, the transformation is not an exact observational equivalence within our fully restricted parametric specification. The zero-mean scrap restriction is an additional transparent normalization adopted for the initial model; it should not be presented as mathematically indispensable under every possible entry-cost specification. It avoids freely estimating the scrap location alongside operating and entry-cost levels and makes the initial parameterization smaller.

This does not prove that the remaining three parameters are separately identified. Before estimation, examine the entry/exit sample, available state variation, chosen moments or likelihood, and sensitivity to the normalization. Sparse actual entry/exit events and the first-incumbent-year calibration proxies remain limitations to discuss with Marc.

### Other confirmed state assumptions

Maintain independent nicotine and $\xi$ transitions across firms, in addition to independence between those two processes within each firm. The industry quality transition kernel is therefore the product of the relevant pooled individual transition probabilities, conditional on the active roster. This rules out common quality/demand innovations in the initial version; private entry/scrap draw independence is a separate maintained assumption.

## 19. Historical market-size path and firms' expectations

For the January 2013–December 2023 historical exercise, use each month's observed market size from the selected demand specification, $M_t=\bar{k}\,\mathrm{Adults}_t$. At each decision date, firms expect that current value to persist at all future horizons:

$$
\mathbb{E}^{\mathrm{firms}}_t[M_{t+h}]=M_t,\qquad h\geq1,
$$

with firms assigning no future variation to market size within that date's stationary problem. When a new annual population-based market size is observed, firms update to the new value and again regard it as permanent. Historical market-size changes are therefore unanticipated revisions under this simplifying belief rule, rather than changes that firms correctly forecast in advance. We are not imposing perfect foresight of the 2013–2023 path or estimating a rational-expectations market-size transition process.

The observed series contains 132 months and eleven distinct annual market-size values, constant within each year. For each trial dynamic payoff vector, solve the stationary entry/exit equilibrium conditional on each distinct $M$. Apply the corresponding policies along the observed historical $M_t$ path during simulation, carrying forward simulated firms and quality states. These are date-specific stationary expectations, rather than a single December-2023 environment for the whole sample or a finite-horizon backward-induction problem.

Continuation values used in month $t$ are calculated under the belief that its own $M_t$ persists. They do not substitute the actual next year's different-$M$ value functions into the current expectation. The following year's policy solution applies only once that revised market size is observed. This distinction keeps computation aligned with the selected expectations rule.

## 25. First manual dynamic entry/exit trial

### Entry includes the option to wait

JUUL and VUSE can reconsider entry each month while they have never entered. Retain that option's value: enter when the current private sunk cost is below $B_e-W_e$, rather than comparing the entry value with zero. A firm that has shut down cannot reenter. Initial quality is drawn after commitment, as agreed above.

The pre-draw value of an eligible candidate is

$$
U_e(\Omega)=\mathbb E_x\left[\max\{W_e(\Omega),B_e(\Omega)-x\}\right].
$$

With $d_e=\max\{B_e-W_e,0\}$ and exponential mean cost $\mu_E$, this becomes

$$
U_e=W_e+d_e-\mu_E\left(1-e^{-d_e/\mu_E}\right),
\qquad p_e=1-e^{-d_e/\mu_E}.
$$

### Trial setup and numerical checks

- Use provisional values $FC=\$100{,}000$ per active month, $\mu_E=\$100$ million, and $\sigma_S=\$5$ million, with mean net scrap zero and monthly discount $0.95^{1/12}$. These are solver trials, not parameter estimates.
- Hold market size and passive-fringe utility at the January 2013 benchmarks. Retain the five fixed brand identities, assigned median costs, independent quality transitions, and the selected entrant initial-state distribution.
- Enumerate 121,000 dynamic states. Each initially active brand has nine quality states plus absorbing shutdown; JUUL and VUSE additionally have a never-entered state. Compute static profits for all 100,000 roster/quality configurations with manual pricing iteration.
- Solve joint value and probability equations manually using damped updates and residual-checked value extrapolation. The extrapolation uses small linear systems; no packaged equilibrium, root-finding, or optimization solver is used.
- **Audit 1: Static pricing.** All configurations pass pricing and share checks; selected configurations also pass independent starting-price and unilateral-deviation checks.
- **Audit 2: Dynamic fixed point.** The maximum scaled Bellman residual is $9.97\times10^{-7}$ and maximum probability residual is $2.67\times10^{-8}$. The Bellman scaling divides by the larger of one dollar and the compared value magnitudes; this is not an absolute dollar error bound.
- **Audit 3: Transition calculations.** Explicit next-state enumeration at selected full-model states agrees with the tensor expectation calculation to relative error below $4.6\times10^{-15}$.
- **Audit 4: Simulated value.** Simulate 5,000 paths for 120 months with independent private draws and terminal continuation values. Simulated discounted payoffs lie within 1.50 Monte Carlo standard errors of computed initial values for all five brands. This checks implementation consistency conditional on the terminal values.

- **Audit 5: Alternative starting point.** A negative-value start with 0.5 initial action probabilities also meets the Bellman and policy tolerances. Across all states, the two solutions differ by at most $3.99\times10^{-6}$ in scaled values and $3.59\times10^{-7}$ in probabilities. This supports numerical robustness to these starts, without proving uniqueness.

At the initial January 2013 state, BLU continues with probability 99.951%; MISTIC and NJOY continue with probability approximately 100%. Conditional entry probabilities are 12.934% for JUUL and 43.285% for VUSE. JUUL's discounted entry value is $78.10 million versus waiting value $64.25 million; VUSE's are $1,559.16 million versus $1,502.44 million. Their cost thresholds are therefore $13.85 million and $56.71 million, respectively.

These are state-dependent monthly probabilities at arbitrary trial parameters. They neither fit observed entry dates nor validate the demand specification, cost assumptions, or fringe benchmark. This stationary trial does not yet simulate or fit the changing 2013--2023 market-size sequence, establish equilibrium uniqueness, or demonstrate identification of the dynamic parameters.
