# Marc meeting - November 16, 2026

## Follow-up: fringe price competition and quality

At the November 10 meeting, Marc asked whether the fringe competed on price and whether it had a quality level. Checking the implementation gives the following answers.

- **Price competition:** The fringe is a passive differentiated alternative. It affects demand and the five focal firms' optimal prices, but it does not choose a price or solve a pricing best response. Its market share responds to changes in focal firms' prices.
- **Observed fringe price:** Total real fringe revenue divided by total fringe liquid volume.
- **Observed fringe quality:** The unweighted mean of UPC nicotine measures among fringe UPCs. An unobserved quality residual is recovered to match observed fringe shares.
- **Implemented dynamic baseline (before the revision below):** Observed fringe price, nicotine, and recovered unobserved quality are bundled into a single mean utility, delta_F. The model holds total fringe mean utility at its January 2013 calibrated value across states and time. The solver does not separately choose or update fringe price or quality; fringe market share remains endogenous.

The observed-data decomposition is delta_F = intercept + nicotine coefficient * fringe nicotine + price coefficient * fringe price + fringe unobserved quality. Total fringe utility is recovered from observed shares; the residual completes this decomposition.

## Provisional decision: observed fringe utility with permanent-current-value beliefs

Decision recorded November 12, 2026, for discussion at the November 16 meeting.

Use the monthly fringe mean utility recovered from demand, $\widehat{\delta}_{F,t}$, for the fixed-focal fringe composition. At each month's decision point, firms observe the current value and believe it will remain at that level forever. When a different value is observed next month, firms revise their beliefs and decisions; these changes are unanticipated under the maintained forecasting rule.

For each distinct monthly fringe utility benchmark, solve the corresponding stationary dynamic game, including continuation values and entry/exit policies. Historical decisions use the policies associated with that month's observed fringe utility. Updating current profits alone while retaining the January-benchmark continuation values would not implement this decision.

Fringe utility remains a fixed parameter within each stationary solve. Under the original fixed-roster specification, this change alone retained **121,000 states per solve**. The subsequent anonymous-population and MC decisions below replace that state count; monthly fringe utility still adds no state dimension within a stationary solve. The fringe remains passive in pricing, and its share continues to respond to focal firms' prices and participation.

This is an intermediate approximation that allows historical fringe competition to vary without giving firms foresight of its realized path. Firms repeatedly revise a permanent-current-value forecast; a stochastic fringe transition matrix remains a possible later extension, not the current decision.

This changes the planned treatment of fringe utility only. It does not adopt a new market-size process or entry specification. The January 2013 constant-fringe solution remains preserved as a comparison baseline. This decision has been recorded but is **not yet implemented in code**. It is Erin's provisional modeling choice, not an extension assigned by Marc in the November 10 meeting.

## Decision: three permanent marginal-cost types

Decision recorded November 12, 2026, for discussion at the November 16 meeting.

Use **three MC support values** to approximate an empirical entrant-cost distribution constructed with equal weight per firm. Each entrant draws MC after committing to entry, independently of its initial nicotine and unobserved quality draws. MC remains fixed for the firm's lifetime. Once revealed, incumbent MCs are public state variables: firms do not integrate over alternative MCs for existing firms, but do integrate over unknown entrant draws.

The equal-weight-per-firm approach and three-value support are adopted. The subsequent approved construction below fixes the empirical sample, within-firm representative, zero-floor order, grouping, support locations and probabilities. Negative-MC concerns remain deferred by agreement with Marc.

With three MC values, three nicotine states and three unobserved-quality states, there are **27 active firm types**. For an anonymous population with at most five active strategic firms, this gives **201,376 public industry configurations**, before bookkeeping for tagged-incumbent values and policies. Different publicly known cost configurations require different values even without uncertainty about current costs. The original 121,000-state fixed-roster count no longer applies to this revised model.

Three MC values are a computational approximation to the empirical distribution. Full-scale runtime remains to be benchmarked; small-game tests do not establish feasibility of all 132 monthly stationary solves. The empirical MC inputs are now constructed as recorded below; no full-scale equilibrium run has been performed.
### Approved empirical construction and results

Approved and constructed November 12, 2026. Use all nine firms in the original observed-market MC recovery (540 firm-months). Calculate each firm's historical median MC, then apply a floor at zero. Give each firm weight 1/9, sort by the floored median, and divide into three consecutive groups of three. Each support value is its group's arithmetic mean; each probability is 1/3. All nine medians are positive, so the floor changes none of these inputs. The extended five-firm recovery remains a possible sensitivity case and is not pooled into this distribution.

## Full five-firm stationary solve attempt

Attempted November 12, 2026, with all 27 types and cap five. Trial payoff settings are FC=100,000/month, mean sunk entry cost=100,000,000 and normal net-scrap SD=5,000,000 with mean zero. Monthly beta=0.95^(1/12). These reuse original trial scales, not revised estimates. MC uses the approved distribution; nicotine/xi representatives and continuing transitions use existing empirical inputs. For this numerical trial only, independent initial nicotine/xi draws use the marginal probabilities of the old empirical joint G. No historical initial incumbent assignment is required to solve the full state space. January 2013 market size and fixed-focal fringe utility are held constant within this trial.

The complete geometry contains **201,376 public configurations** and **849,555 occupied tagged-type entries**. Geometry took 4.4-4.9 seconds and about 70 MB in R. A vectorized manual pricing implementation completed all configurations in 7.7-10.8 seconds. Maximum static FOC residual was 5.66e-12; mass checks passed. Selected singleton and full markets were checked against the existing independent scalar fixed-point and unilateral bisection routines. Static profits are saved for reuse.

**The dynamic game did not converge: neither attempt completed its first Bellman update within the imposed 60-second update limit.** No dynamic residual or converged solution is available. This is a bounded computational attempt, not evidence that no equilibrium exists or that a longer run cannot finish.

The first attempt revealed linear named-vector state lookup as a bottleneck. A hash lookup was added, and the independent-enumeration and cached/reference suites passed. On retry, the partial cache reached 293 completed keys, 667,770 successor entries and about 10.5 MB payload before the same time limit (versus 27 keys in the first attempt). This improves construction throughput but does not establish feasibility of caching every full-state successor distribution. The 2,000,000-entry cache budget was not the stopping condition in either attempt.
